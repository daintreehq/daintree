## Context

`ResourceGovernor` (`electron/pty-host/ResourceGovernor.ts`) runs in the pty-host utility process and polls `process.memoryUsage().heapUsed` against `v8.getHeapStatistics().heap_size_limit` every 2s (`CHECK_INTERVAL_MS`). At 85% (`MEMORY_LIMIT_PERCENT`) it pauses all PTYs via each terminal's `PtyPauseCoordinator` ("resource-governor" token); it resumes when raw util drops below 60% (`RESUME_THRESHOLD_PERCENT`) or after a bounded `FORCE_RESUME_MS=10s` safety timeout.

## Problem

Under sustained memory pressure the governor flapped: raw heap readings crossed 85% and paused everything, the 10s force-resume timeout then resumed _regardless of utilization_, and ~2s later raw heap was still high so it re-paused. Terminals (including active agents) were repeatedly hard-paused/resumed in a ~12s cycle (issue #8616).

## Root cause

Two compounding traps, both on the raw `heapUsed` value:

1. **`heapUsed` is a sawtooth.** V8 old-gen allocation climbs until a GC, then drops; a single tick can read a transient pre-GC spike far above the true working-set. Comparing the _raw_ per-tick value against a fixed threshold means transient spikes trip the pause with no signal conditioning. There is no hysteresis on the _signal_, only on the engage/resume _band_.
2. **The bounded-pause force-resume is the second half of the loop.** The `FORCE_RESUME_MS` timeout is a liveness guarantee (don't pause forever), but it resumed _unconditionally_ — including when pressure had not cleared. So pause→(10s)→force-resume→(2s)→re-pause is a stable oscillation, not an edge case.

## Lesson

When making throttle/pause decisions from a raw V8 heap metric, condition the signal and gate re-engagement — do not threshold the raw per-tick value:

- **Smooth the engage signal with an EMA.** `EMA_ALPHA = 2/(N+1)` (here `2/11` ≈ a 20s window at 2s polls) rejects single-tick GC sawtooth while still detecting sustained pressure in ~4–5 ticks. Seed the EMA with the _first real reading_, not 0, or you get a false-low warmup ramp.
- **Add a warmup gate.** `WARMUP_TICKS=5` suppresses engage until the EMA has stabilized, so a startup heap spike can't pause everything.
- **Add a re-engage cooldown after any force-resume.** `REENGAGE_COOLDOWN_MS=30s` blocks re-engagement even if pressure persists — terminals keep running (possibly slowly under OS backpressure) instead of re-pausing. This is what breaks the flap loop; smoothing alone does not.
- **Engage on the smoothed value, but disengage on the RAW value.** Resume should be prompt when pressure truly clears; the 85→60% hysteresis band plus the cooldown already prevent a single low tick from oscillating. Mixing smoothed-engage / raw-disengage is intentional, not inconsistent.
- **Keep a critical bypass.** At `CRITICAL_PERCENT=95` raw, skip EMA/warmup/cooldown and pause immediately — the next allocation could OOM.
- **Pre-pause trim is one-shot and lags one tick.** Calling `trimBuffers()` (wired in `pty-host.ts` to `ptyManager.trimScrollback(SCROLLBACK_MIN)`) drops JS references synchronously but `heapUsed` won't fall until the next GC, so you wait a tick before escalating to pause. Guard it with a `trimAttemptedForCurrentPressure` flag and re-arm it when pressure clears, or you thrash scrollback every tick.
- The warning band stays on raw util (advisory, smoothing would only add lag); resume ordering is idle-first/active-last so working agents get the most heap runway.

## Applies when

- Touching `ResourceGovernor` engage/disengage/trim logic, its thresholds, or its EMA/warmup/cooldown constants.
- Writing any code that makes a control decision (pause, throttle, evict, GC, downgrade profile) from `process.memoryUsage().heapUsed` or `v8.getHeapStatistics()` — expect a pre-GC sawtooth and condition the signal.
- Adding a "bounded" safety timeout that auto-resumes/auto-recovers a paused state: verify the recovery checks the underlying condition or is gated by a cooldown, or it becomes half of a flap loop.
- Debugging reports of terminals/agents repeatedly pausing and resuming, or `host-throttled` events oscillating, under memory pressure.
- Tuning the pty-host memory governor alongside `ResourceProfileService` profile hysteresis (#4629) — these are different layers (signal smoothing + re-engage gate here vs. time-based profile transitions there).
