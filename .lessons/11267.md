## Context

When a worktree is deleted externally its surviving terminals get parked in a ghost row in the sidebar (`DeletedWorktreeCard`) with an auto-close countdown, the only rescue surface for dragging those terminals elsewhere before `sweepDeletedWorktreeCleanup` (1Hz, `src/store/deletedWorktreeCleanup.ts`) moves them into trash. #11237 introduced the countdown; #11267 rewrote its timing after issue #11259 found it broken both ways; #11266 (grouped rows) and #11275 (rescue-follow selection) round out the same rescue-surface arc and are unaffected by the timing bug itself.

## Problem

The countdown failed in two opposite, both-bad, ways. It could hold a row open forever: an agent stuck in `working`, or a leaked app-wide drag flag, made `isCountdownDeferred` re-arm the deadline to a fresh full TTL on every tick instead of pausing it, so the progress bar snapped to full with nothing in the row explaining why. Or it could fire with zero rescue window: the deadline was a wall-clock timestamp stamped at deletion time, so a project view that went cached/backgrounded/minimized right after deletion thawed with an already-expired deadline and silently trashed live agent sessions on the very first tick back. The second failure is the dangerous one — no crash, no error, just gone.

## Root cause

Two platform lies made wall-clock timing look safe when it wasn't. First, a cached `WebContentsView` (Electron's `removeChildView` + `setVisible(false)`, per `ProjectViewLifecycleController.deactivateEntry`) never flips `document.visibilityState` for that renderer — Chromium tracks occlusion at the `BrowserWindow` level, so `document.hidden` stays false and a `visibilitychange` catch-up never fires, even though the view is fully frozen (`src/lib/viewCacheState.ts`, #11212). Second, a minimized-but-not-cached window IS correctly reported as hidden, but Chromium's first background timer throttle tier caps interval timers at 1Hz — exactly this sweep's own `DELETED_WORKTREE_SWEEP_INTERVAL_MS` (1000ms) — so "hidden means the sweep stops" is false too: it keeps firing on schedule and spends the countdown behind a window nobody can see. Neither signal implies the other, so the fix checks both (`deps.isViewCached() || deps.isViewHidden()`); checking only one leaves the other path free to silently trash a row.

The fix replaces the wall-clock deadline with an observed-time model. Rows are recorded unarmed and only armed by a sweep that is actually running; a gap between sweeps longer than `MAX_OBSERVED_SWEEP_GAP_MS` (2x the interval) is credited back to every armed deadline instead of spent — one mechanism covering a cached view, a hidden window, a stalled renderer, and a sleeping machine — while every sweep still spends at least one interval, so a run of just-over-threshold gaps can't stall the countdown indefinitely. Holds (drag/confirm/working-agent) now pin the _remaining_ time (`heldRemaining`, captured once per hold) instead of resetting to a fresh TTL every tick, and each hold reason gets a cap (`DELETED_WORKTREE_TRANSIENT_HOLD_CAP_MS` = 5min for drag/confirm, `DELETED_WORKTREE_AGENT_HOLD_CAP_MS` = 60min for a working agent) so a leaked or wedged hold can no longer suppress the countdown forever — it falls through to a lower-precedence still-eligible reason instead of masking it.

## Lesson

- **Never stamp a destructive deadline in wall-clock time if the observer can be frozen.** Measure and charge only the time the row could actually have been on screen, crediting unobserved gaps back rather than spending them — this turns "silently trash the row" into "the countdown resumes where it left off."
- **A cached `WebContentsView` still reports `visible`** — `document.visibilityState`/`visibilitychange` are dead signals for that case (see `src/lib/viewCacheState.ts`). Any renderer-side periodic or lifecycle logic gating on visibility alone needs the explicit `isProjectViewCached()` check too; the two checks are not redundant, AND them, don't pick one.
- **A minimized/hidden window is throttled, not stopped.** Chromium's first background timer tier is exactly 1Hz, so a 1-second sweep interval looks like it's "working fine" (firing right on schedule) while still doing something destructive behind glass — don't assume `hidden` implies "paused," check what the callback actually does when it fires.
- **Re-arming a paused deadline to a fresh full TTL on every tick is functionally "never expires."** If a hold is meant to pause a countdown, capture the remaining value ONCE at hold-start and pin it, not recompute-and-reset each tick; cap every hold condition too, even ones that "should" be long-lived like a working agent, since any hold with no owner to clear it can leak and hold forever.
- **When two failure modes are opposite (never-fires vs. fires-instantly), fixing one naively risks recreating the other.** This PR's own adversarial review passes caught two more bugs of exactly that shape mid-fix — a hidden-window path still spending countdown on schedule, and a gap-crediting scheme that could zero out forever against a run of just-over-threshold gaps. Budget for a second adversarial pass on any interval-timer fix, checked against both directions.

## Applies when

- Touching `src/store/deletedWorktreeCleanup.ts`, `sweepDeletedWorktreeCleanup`, or any other periodic sweep that guards a destructive action behind a countdown or TTL.
- Adding or debugging renderer-side logic that gates on `document.visibilityState`/`visibilitychange` for a per-project `WebContentsView` — check `src/lib/viewCacheState.ts` first.
- Diagnosing "a background/minimized window did something it shouldn't have while nobody was looking" — suspect Chromium's background timer throttle tier (1Hz) rather than assuming timers stop entirely.
- Reviewing any hold/defer/pause mechanism on a countdown or TTL — verify it captures remaining time once and pins it, rather than recomputing a fresh full duration on every check.
- Designing a rescue window before a destructive auto-sweep (auto-trash, auto-delete, auto-archive) — wall-clock deadlines are unsafe wherever the observer (tab, view, process) can be suspended or cached.
