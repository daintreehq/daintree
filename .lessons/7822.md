## Context

The Review Hub surfaces a "push rejected (non-fast-forward)" banner with inline recovery CTAs: "Pull and rebase" and "Force push". Force push runs through the `git:force-push-with-lease` IPC handler in `electron/ipc/handlers/git-write.ts`, which executes `git push origin <branch> --force-with-lease=<branch>:<sha> --force-if-includes`. Daintree also runs a continuous background fetch loop in the workspace-host (`FetchScheduler` → `RepoFetchCoordinator` → `WorktreeMonitor.scheduleFetch`) that periodically advances `refs/remotes/origin/<branch>` so ahead/behind counts stay fresh.

## Problem

A plain `git push --force-with-lease` (no explicit `<ref>:<expected-sha>`) computes its safety lease against the _current_ local remote-tracking ref. In an app that fetches in the background, the window between "push rejected" and "user clicks Force push" is long enough for `FetchScheduler` to fetch and advance `refs/remotes/origin/<branch>` to exactly the upstream commits that caused the rejection. The lease then matches the freshly-fetched ref and passes — silently degrading `--force-with-lease` into an effective `--force` that clobbers a teammate's just-pushed work, which is the exact data-loss outcome the lease exists to prevent.

## Root cause

The lease is only meaningful if it pins the SHA the user actually saw/decided against. The protective value is the _timing_ of the SHA capture, not the flag name. `handlePush` captures `git revparse refs/remotes/origin/<branch>` at the moment of rejection and threads it through the thrown `GitOperationError` as `leaseSha` + `branchName`. Those fields are promoted to top-level slots on `SerializedError` (`shared/types/ipc/errors.ts`) so they survive the packaged-build error strip and the cross-realm IPC envelope (`shared/utils/ipcErrorSerialization.ts`, and the `[GitError|reason|leaseSha|branchName]` realm-safe decode in `src/utils/clientGitError.ts`). The renderer reads them back off `pushError` and passes the _original_ SHA unchanged into `forcePushWithLease` — never re-querying git at click time. `--force-if-includes` is added as a second guard against the reflog-based "did your local include the remote tip you're overwriting" check.

## Lesson

- Never expose plain `--force-with-lease` (no explicit `<ref>:<sha>`) in any app that fetches in the background — the lease is computed against a ref a fetch can move. Always use the `--force-with-lease=<branch>:<sha>` form with a SHA captured at decision time, plus `--force-if-includes`.
- Capture the lease SHA at the moment the operation is _rejected/decided_, in the main process, and carry it forward as immutable data. Do not re-derive `refs/remotes/origin/<branch>` later — any re-query reintroduces the race.
- When threading a captured value through an IPC error, promote it to an explicit top-level field on `SerializedError` (and the realm-safe encode/decode path). Errors lose ad-hoc properties across the packaged-build strip and structuredClone/cross-realm boundary; sub-string-parsing the message is not durable.
- Validate the SHA in the handler (`/^[0-9a-f]{4,64}$/i`) before interpolating into `--force-with-lease=<branch>:<sha>` — the ref:sha form must never accept arbitrary input that could smuggle a `--flag`.
- If the SHA can't be captured (e.g. no upstream tracking, `revparse` throws), omit it and suppress the force-push CTA rather than falling back to a lease-less force.

## Applies when

- Adding or modifying any force-push / `--force-with-lease` path, especially user-facing "force push" CTAs in the Review Hub or worktree dashboard.
- Touching `electron/ipc/handlers/git-write.ts`, the `git:force-push-with-lease` / `git:push` handlers, or `ForcePushConfirmDialog.tsx`.
- Working on push-rejection / `push-rejected-outdated` divergence-recovery UI, or any "discarded remote commits" preview.
- Any feature that reads `refs/remotes/origin/<branch>` to make a safety decision while `FetchScheduler` / `RepoFetchCoordinator` background fetches can advance remote-tracking refs underneath you.
- Threading a captured value (SHA, branch, token) through a thrown `GitOperationError` / `SerializedError` across the main↔renderer IPC boundary — confirm it survives the packaged-build strip and structuredClone realm crossing.
